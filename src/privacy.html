Privacy policy

Effective November 13, 2026 (crash and error reports; push notifications, account restrictions; earlier: emails about your reviews and account, Helpful votes, public names, reviewer profiles, photo credits and photo reports)

The short version. Gracio collects what it needs to run: your sign-in details, your profile and what you post. Your location is used to sort places by distance and is not stored. Every review is checked before it's posted: people's names are hidden automatically, which means the text of your comment is sent to Anthropic's Claude AI for that check. There are no ads and no third-party trackers, and your personal data is never sold. You can delete your account and everything you posted at any time.

Who we are

Gracio ("we") is an independent app for rating local businesses, made in Bergen County, New Jersey. Questions about privacy: privacy@gracioapp.com.

What we collect

Your account

When you sign in with Apple or Google, we receive your name and email address. If you use Apple's "Hide My Email", we get a private relay address instead. We never see your Apple or Google password.

Your profile

Your first name or nickname, an optional last name, a short "about" line, your avatar color, your favorite categories, your preferred time filter and whether your profile is public or private. Names are checked when you save them: numbers, email addresses, links, emoji and official-looking names (like "Gracio Support") aren't allowed. You can change your name once every 7 days. We keep a private record of each name change (the old and new public name and the date) so moderators can look into impersonation or abuse; it is never shown to other people.

What you post

Your ratings, detail ratings, comments, updates and shout-outs; photos you add to a review (up to 10 per review or update, each with the type you pick, like "Food" or "Inside"); the collections you make (if you save places to Favorites without an account, they're kept only on your phone until you sign in, then added to your account); businesses you suggest; reports you send; and the people you block. The Helpful or Not helpful votes you give to other people's reviews.

Photos

Gracio only gets the photos you pick or take for a review. Before uploading, the app makes each photo smaller and saves it again as a new image, which leaves out hidden details stored in the original file, such as where the photo was taken.

Messages from Gracio

Messages we send you in the app, for example when your review is approved or not, when a business you suggested is added, or when a report you sent is decided.

We also email you at the address on your account: about your reviews (when one is published or couldn't be posted) and about your account (for example, when a review or photo is removed, your profile is reset or your account is suspended or restored). You can turn off the emails about your reviews in Settings → Notifications → Review updates → Email; emails about your account are always sent. We don't send marketing emails. We keep a record of the emails we sent (which message, to which account, and whether it was delivered, but not your email address) for 90 days.

Push notifications (only if you turn them on)

If you turn on notifications, Gracio saves your phone's notification address (a "push token") with your account so we can tell you when a review you posted is published or can't be posted, and about important changes to your account. Notifications are delivered through Expo's push service and then Apple or Google, which receive the notification address and the short text of the notification. We don't use notifications for advertising or marketing, and we don't send text messages. You can turn notifications off in Settings → Notifications or in your phone's settings. The address is removed when you sign out, when your phone stops accepting notifications, and when you delete your account. We keep a record of the notifications we sent, without the address, for 90 days.

Account restrictions

If an account breaks our rules we may suspend it. We tell you the general reason and when it ends, in the app and by email. Important messages about your account are always emailed and can't be turned off. To appeal, email support@gracioapp.com.

Your location (only if you allow it)

Your phone's location is used to sort places nearest first and, when you post a review, to check whether you're within 1,000 feet of the business (the "Verified" mark). To find nearby places, the app sends your approximate location (rounded to about 10 meters) along with the search. We don't store your location. For reviews we keep only whether the review was Verified. Gracio works with location turned off; places are then sorted from Bergenfield, NJ.

Address search (when you add a business)

When you type an address in "Add a business", the text you type is sent through our server to Geoapify, our address search service, which sends back matching addresses. We don't send your name, email address or account details with it. If you've allowed location, we may also send your approximate position (rounded to about 1 kilometer) so nearby addresses come first. When you pick an address, its street, town, state, ZIP code and map coordinates are saved with your suggestion. If a business you suggested is added, that address and location become part of its public listing. If you'd rather not use address search, you can type the address in yourself.

Game scores

When you're signed in, your best score in each game is saved with your account and shown on the game leaderboard. When you're signed out, it's kept only on your phone.

Crash and error reports

If the app crashes or hits an error, it sends a report to Sentry, our error-reporting service, so we can fix the problem. A report has what went wrong and where in the app's code, the app version, and your phone's model and iOS version. It doesn't include your name, email address, account, location, reviews or photos, and we've set Sentry not to keep IP addresses. We don't record your screen or what you tap, and we don't use these reports for advertising or to follow what you do.

Technical logs

Like most online services, our database provider keeps short-lived server logs (for example, IP address and the time of a request) for security and troubleshooting.

What we don't collect

Your contacts, your photo library (only the photos you choose), health data or advertising ID. Gracio has no ads, no third-party analytics, no screen recording and no tracking across other apps or websites.

How we use it

  • To run the app: show places, your reviews and collections, and calculate Gracio scores.
  • To keep Gracio fair and safe: daily limits, the Verified and Trusted marks, checking reviews before they're posted, and reviewing reports.
  • To answer you when you contact us.

How reviews are checked

A Gracio moderator approves every review, update and photo before anyone else can see it. Until then, only you can see it, and it doesn't count toward scores. A moderator may leave out photos that break our rules.

When you post, a few things are hidden automatically and shown as ***:

  • People's names. The text of your comment is sent to Claude, an AI service from Anthropic, which finds the names of people in it (staff, customers or anyone else). Names of businesses, brands and dishes stay. Claude also points out anything a moderator should look at, like a threat or someone's personal information. Only the comment text is sent: not your name, email address or location.
  • Phone numbers, email addresses and links, in comments, "about" lines and collection names.
  • Slurs, in anything you post.

So a moderator can fix a mistake (for example, a word that was hidden but isn't a name), we keep what you typed privately until your review is approved or not, and then delete it. Your private shout-out to staff is not sent to Claude.

What other people can see

Public: your public name (your first name or nickname and your last initial, like "John S."; your full last name is never shown), your avatar color and "about" line, your approved reviews, updates and photos (each photo shows the public name of the person who posted it), your public collections (anyone with a collection's link can see its name and places, but not who made it; it isn't listed or searchable, and making it private again closes the link), whether you're a Trusted reviewer, and, if you play while signed in, your best game scores on the leaderboard.

Your reviewer profile. Tapping your name on a review opens a profile page that shows your public name, your avatar, the month you joined, whether you're a Trusted reviewer, how many reviews you've published and those reviews with their approved photos. It never shows your email address, last name, location, saved places or collections, or anything waiting for approval. New profiles are public. You can make yours private in Settings → Privacy → Public profile: the page then shows only your name. Each approved review still appears on its business page with your public name either way, so a private profile doesn't make your reviews anonymous. Profiles that existed before November 11, 2026 started private.

Helpful votes. Each review shows how many people marked it Helpful. Nobody can see who voted, and the number of Not helpful votes is never shown to the public. Gracio moderators can see both totals. You can change or remove your vote at any time, and you can't vote on your own reviews. Votes don't change a business's score.

Private: your email address, your last name, your settings, your Helpful and Not helpful votes, your private collections, reviews and photos waiting for approval or not approved, your messages from Gracio, the businesses you suggest, the reports you send and the people you block (they aren't told). Gracio moderators can see reviews waiting for approval, suggestions and reports in order to review them.

Who we share it with

We never sell your personal data and we don't share it with advertisers. We use a few service providers to run Gracio, and they handle data only to provide their service to us:

  • Supabase: database, photo storage and sign-in (servers in the United States).
  • Anthropic: Claude, which checks the text of review comments for people's names and possible problems when you post (see "How reviews are checked").
  • Apple and Google: Sign in with Apple and Sign in with Google.
  • Geoapify: address search. Finds matching addresses when you add a business (see "Address search"). Address data is from OpenStreetMap contributors.
  • Resend: delivers the emails we send you (see "Messages from Gracio"). It receives your email address, your first name and the message so it can deliver it.
  • Expo: delivers push notifications, if you turn them on, through Apple or Google (see "Push notifications"). It receives your phone's notification address and the short text of the notification. Expo also delivers updates to the app; like any download, that request includes your phone's IP address.
  • Sentry: crash and error reports (see "Crash and error reports").
  • Google Workspace: our email, if you write to us.

We may also share information if the law requires it, or to protect people from harm.

How long we keep it

We keep your information while you have an account. What you typed before names were hidden is deleted as soon as a moderator decides on your review, and photos that aren't approved are deleted. When you delete your account, we delete your profile, reviews, photos, collections, suggestions, reports, blocks, votes, game scores, messages, email records, notification addresses and records, and settings. If you signed in with Apple, we also ask Apple to end Gracio's access to your Apple ID. A record of moderation decisions (for example, that a review or photo was hidden for breaking the rules) may be kept to prevent abuse. Photos a moderator hides after a report are kept hidden, not public, until the review is deleted or the decision is reversed. The record of your name changes is deleted with your account. Server logs expire on their own.

Your choices

  • Edit your name and profile, and make your profile and collections private, at any time.
  • Turn off emails or push notifications about your reviews, and push notifications about your account, in Settings → Notifications. Emails about your account are always sent.
  • Change or remove a Helpful or Not helpful vote at any time.
  • Report a photo that's yours, breaks the rules or shouldn't be there: open it in the app and tap Report. A moderator may remove it.
  • Turn location off in your phone's settings.
  • Delete your account in the app (Profile → Settings → Delete account) or by emailing privacy@gracioapp.com.
  • Ask for a copy of your data, or for us to correct it, by emailing us. We answer within 30 days.

Children

Gracio is not meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their information. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, email us and we'll delete it.

Security

All connections to Gracio are encrypted, and database rules make sure only you can change your own content. No system is perfectly secure, so we also keep what we collect to a minimum.

Changes

If we change this policy, we'll update this page and the date above. For important changes, we'll also let you know in the app.
